3. Season Energy Zone Plan

Generate a plan to see this.
Yardage alone doesn't guarantee the intended effect for SP1-3, especially SP2 — true lactate-production work needs long, near-full recovery between reps (often several minutes, sometimes much longer) to stay maximal. The same yardage with short rest becomes lactate-tolerance work instead, a different target. REC yardage matters too, not just as filler — active recovery clears lactate faster than passive rest and is part of what makes the rest of the week's volume sustainable. Day sequencing still matters, but the mechanism is metabolic, not the land-sprint CNS-fatigue rule it's sometimes borrowed from: water removes most of the ground-impact/eccentric loading that drives multi-day neuromuscular deficits on land, and research on swimmers racing across multi-day meets shows jump/neuromuscular output holding up even as lactate and perceived effort climb. So back-to-back SP1-3 days aren't the same 48-72hr CNS risk they'd be on land — but the metabolic cost (lactate, glycogen, RPE) is real and still needs the recovery to actually happen, so keep some easier volume around genuinely hard days rather than stacking them purely for CNS-timing reasons.
(Weekly zone splits above are season-level targets, not a day-by-day schedule — how you sequence SP-zone sessions within the week is still on the coach.)
